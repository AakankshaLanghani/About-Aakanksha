Featured research · 2026

EEG-Based Emotion Recognition for Clinical Assistive Healthcare

Patients with locked-in syndrome, end-stage ALS, or severe spinal cord injury are fully cognitively aware but have no way to express pain, fear, or discomfort. I built an end-to-end brain-computer interface for real-time emotion recognition from EEG signals — 14-channel data, four-stage filtering, 11 features per epoch across five frequency bands. Six classifiers trained on 13,068 windowed samples; XGBoost won with 90% accuracy and 91.5% precision on the Stress/Pain class — the one that matters most clinically.
